In a statutory appraisal following M.P.M.'s sale to an industrial buyer, the corporation argued that the Court of Chancery had to give weight to the merger terms and two earlier offers. The Delaware Supreme Court affirmed a DCF-based appraisal that assigned those transactions no weight. Although arm's-length market values can be reliable evidence, the proponent had not established a nexus between the offers and M.P.M.'s going-concern value at the merger date.

The opinion draws the line directly: section 262 values the company to the stockholder as a going concern, not to a third party as an acquisition, and excludes merger-created synergies. A buyer-side DCF designed to give all synergies to the seller was therefore impermissible. *M.P.M.* is a material limitation on any claim that the strategic buyer itself defines Delaware fair value.
