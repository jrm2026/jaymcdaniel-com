*Dugan* is filed under matrimonial law and belongs in every business divorce practitioner's working set, because it draws the line that determines how much of a professional practice is worth anything at all. Goodwill is reputation likely to generate future business. The distributable portion is the goodwill that exists as a going concern independent of the individual — value a successor could buy and keep.

That distinction, personal goodwill against enterprise goodwill, does the work that key-person discounts are usually asked to do, and it does it at the right point in the analysis. A key-person discount reduces the value of an asset the entity owns, and it invites the response that the person is not actually leaving. A personal goodwill argument says the value was never the entity's to begin with. Absent an enforceable covenant not to compete, the practitioner takes it with him.

The practical consequence in a single-rainmaker firm is large enough to swallow the discount fight entirely. Two competent appraisers who agree on every multiple, every rate, and every normalization adjustment will still be far apart if one treats the founder's client relationships as institutional and the other treats them as personal. Establish which they are — through referral source documentation, contract assignability, and what happened when other producers left — before arguing about percentages.
