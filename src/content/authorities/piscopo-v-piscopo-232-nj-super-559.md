*Piscopo* carried *Dugan* out of the professional office and into celebrity, and in doing so it showed how far the reputation-generates-patronage principle reaches. The performer's reputation is itself the asset, measured by past earning capacity and the probability that past earnings continue. The Appellate Division held that goodwill of this kind is distributable and that the difficulty of valuing it does not affect whether it is included in the marital estate.

The case matters for the ordinary practice dispute even though its facts are unusual. It confirms that *Dugan* is not confined to law and medicine: any individual whose established reputation produces probable future patronage can hold distributable goodwill, and a court cannot avoid the question by calling the number hard to prove. What varies from case to case is not includability but measurement — and measurement returns to the same excess-earnings inquiry *Dugan* and *Levy* prescribe.
