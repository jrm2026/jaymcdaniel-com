The dispute required the court to value an ownership interest in the closely held AriZona beverage business. The court used a discounted-cash-flow framework and declined to adopt preliminary expressions of acquisition interest as transaction value because they never matured into bona fide offers and did not establish what a buyer would actually pay on the valuation date.

The court applied a 25% discount for lack of marketability. Its conclusion rested on a collection of company-specific risks, including transfer restrictions, pending litigation, the absence of audited financial statements, tax uncertainty, and the limited reliability of prior indications of interest. The decision is an unreported trial-level ruling. It is useful as a fact-intensive example, but it should not be reduced to a rule that failed buyer interest alone determines the discount.
