Hewlett-Packard acquired Aruba after an arm's-length process. In the appraisal proceeding, the Court of Chancery selected Aruba's unaffected thirty-day average market price, which was below both the deal price and the parties' valuation evidence. The Delaware Supreme Court reversed, concluding that the chosen market-price metric lacked adequate record support and that the deal process provided persuasive evidence of value.

The Court directed entry of judgment at $19.10 per share—the deal price less the value attributed to synergies. *Verition* therefore shows that an actual strategic transaction can be the best valuation evidence while preserving section 262's exclusion of buyer-created value. It supports using the market transaction as a starting point, not treating the strategic buyer's full investment value as statutory fair value.
