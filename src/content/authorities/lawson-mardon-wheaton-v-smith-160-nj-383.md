The corporation restructured in a manner that triggered dissenters' rights and then sought to purchase the dissenters' stock with a discount for lack of marketability. The Supreme Court held that such a discount is ordinarily unavailable absent extraordinary circumstances because it would penalize the dissenters, benefit the controlling owners, and encourage squeeze-outs.

The Court also directed the trial court to reopen the record to consider a merger price paid approximately four and one-half years after the valuation date. The later transaction did not automatically establish fair value. It was potentially probative evidence whose significance had to be assessed on remand. The distinction matters: *Lawson* permits real transaction evidence to test an appraisal, but it does not hold that every strategic acquisition price—or any buyer-specific synergy embedded in it—equals statutory fair value.
