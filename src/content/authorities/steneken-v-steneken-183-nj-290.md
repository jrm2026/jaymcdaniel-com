*Steneken* is the double-dip case, and it matters wherever a goodwill number and an alimony number are built from the same earnings. The question was whether it is impermissible double counting to value a closely held business using normalized owner compensation — adding back the amount by which the owner overpays himself — while simultaneously setting alimony from his actual, higher income. The Court held that it is not. Actual income remains the lodestar for alimony, and normalizing compensation for valuation purposes serves a different, legitimate end.

The holding is not a green light, though, and the limiting principle is the useful part. The interplay between alimony and equitable distribution is subject to an overarching concept of fairness, given the interrelated yet separate purposes the two serve. The court may use both, but it must attend to the combined result.

Read alongside *Slutsky*, the point sharpens to a practice rule. The excess earnings capitalized into a goodwill figure are the same earnings that will fund alimony for years. Nothing forbids using both — but everything requires the court to explain why the combined burden is fair, and that fairness assessment has to be requested. Counsel who does not put the argument on the record has waived the most valuable adjustment available.
