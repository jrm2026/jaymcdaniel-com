*Seiler* is the entity-type counterexample that keeps the goodwill inquiry honest. The husband ran an exclusive Allstate agency, but Allstate owned the equipment, assigned the telephone number, designed the advertising, and controlled hiring. Goodwill existed — the agency was a going concern with real reputation — but it belonged to Allstate, not to the agent. There was nothing distributable in the husband's hands.

The lesson is that ownership of the goodwill is a threshold question, prior to any valuation method. Before an appraiser capitalizes a dollar of excess earnings, someone has to establish that the goodwill being valued is the spouse's to keep. A captive agent, a franchisee on a tight leash, or a producer whose book of business reverts to the firm on departure may generate substantial enterprise value that is simply not his. *Seiler* is the case to cite when the other side's expert has valued goodwill that the marital owner never owned.
