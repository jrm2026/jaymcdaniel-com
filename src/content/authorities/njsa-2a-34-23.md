Section 2A:34-23 is the source of both alimony and equitable distribution in New Jersey. On divorce the court may effect an equitable distribution of "the property, both real and personal, which was legally and beneficially acquired by [the parties] or either of them during the marriage." The triggering condition is acquisition during the marriage; title and the older notion of "vesting" do not control.

"Property" is read comprehensively — it reaches intangibles, including the goodwill of a professional practice. But it has an outer bound the goodwill cases turn on: a spouse's earning capacity, however enhanced by the other spouse, is not itself property subject to distribution. *Stern v. Stern* drew that line, and it is the line every professional-practice valuation is really arguing about — how much of the number is distributable goodwill and how much is the non-distributable value of the owner's own labor.

The statute fixes the standard as an *equitable* distribution — equitable, not necessarily equal. Its companion section, N.J.S.A. 2A:34-23.1, supplies the factors the court must weigh and the findings it must make in exercising that discretion.
