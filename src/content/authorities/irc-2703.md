Section 2703 is the provision that decides whether the restrictions generating a discount are respected at all, and it operates before any appraisal question is reached. Added as part of Chapter 14 in 1990, it directs that for estate and gift tax purposes the value of property be determined without regard to two things: any option, agreement, or other right to acquire or use the property at a price less than fair market value, and any restriction on the right to sell or use the property.

Subsection (b) supplies the exception, and it is conjunctive. The arrangement must be a bona fide business arrangement; it must not be a device to transfer the property to members of the family for less than full and adequate consideration in money or money's worth; and its terms must be comparable to similar arrangements entered into by persons in an arm's-length transaction. Each requirement is independently satisfied or the exception fails, which means a buy-sell that is commercially sensible in isolation still fails if its terms are not what unrelated parties would have agreed.

The practical consequence for a closely held owner is that the appraisal is downstream. Where an entity was formed weeks before a transfer, holds nothing but marketable securities, has no operations, and serves no purpose the family can articulate other than tax, the restrictions producing the discount are the ones § 2703(a) tells the appraiser to ignore. A better report does not rescue that structure — if the restriction is disregarded by statute, the discount attached to it goes with it.

The mirror image is where the section rewards planning. An operating agreement that predates the transfer by years, reflects a real governance arrangement among people who actually run a business together, and carries terms an unrelated buyer would have accepted, is the arrangement § 2703(b) is written to protect. The same document is doing double duty: it is the restriction that supports the discount and the evidence that the restriction is genuine. Section 2704 addresses a narrower category of liquidation restrictions in family-controlled entities and should be read alongside it.
