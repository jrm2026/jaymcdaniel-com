Section 2A:34-23.1 governs how the equitable-distribution discretion granted by 2A:34-23 is exercised. It lists the factors the court must consider — the duration of the marriage, the parties' economic circumstances and needs, each spouse's contribution to the acquisition and appreciation of marital property, and more — and it directs that "the court shall make specific findings of fact on the evidence relevant to all issues pertaining to asset eligibility or ineligibility, asset valuation, and equitable distribution." Equitable is not synonymous with equal.

Factor (p), the catch-all — "any other factors which the court may deem relevant" — is where the goodwill cases do their fairness work. In *Slutsky v. Slutsky* the Appellate Division invoked (p) to hold that "a measure of consideration must be given to the lack of intrinsic value associated with any amount determined as individual goodwill": the court may award the non-titled spouse less than half of an asset the titled spouse can never sell and must fund with cash. The statute is the authority for that adjustment, and it is the answer to the equity problem *Dugan* identified — a titled spouse "forced to pay the ex-spouse 'tangible' dollars for an intangible asset." Counsel who wants the adjustment has to request it and build the record for it.
