Two discounts do most of the work in closely held valuation. The minority discount reflects that a non-controlling stake cannot direct the company. The marketability discount reflects that shares in a private business cannot be readily sold. Apply both and a minority interest can be worth a fraction of its pro-rata share of enterprise value. Disallow them and it is worth its full proportion.

Which applies is not an appraisal judgment. It is a legal one, set by the standard of value the forum uses. Fair market value, the willing-buyer-willing-seller standard, generally permits both discounts. Fair value — the standard most states apply in oppression, dissolution, and dissenters'-rights cases — usually does not, on the theory that a wrongfully squeezed-out owner should not absorb a discount created by the squeeze.

Because the swing is so large, the standard-of-value question is frequently the whole case. Establish that fair value governs, and the minority owner recovers a proportionate share. Establish fair market value, and the discounts return, often cutting the recovery by a third or more.

Litigants who treat this as a technical afterthought lose it. The parties who win have decided, early and deliberately, which standard the facts and the forum support — and have built the record to get there before the appraisers ever run a number.
