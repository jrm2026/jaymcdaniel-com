Gift-tax valuation is where aggressive planning meets a motivated examiner. Owners transfer minority interests in a family entity, apply discounts for lack of control and marketability, and report a value well below the pro-rata share of the underlying assets. The discounts are legitimate. Whether they hold depends almost entirely on the quality of the record built at the time of the gift.

A defensible file starts with a qualified appraisal by an independent, credentialed appraiser, prepared contemporaneously with the transfer. It states the standard of value, identifies the interest transferred, and supports each discount with data — restricted-stock and closed-end-fund studies for marketability, control-premium evidence for the minority discount — rather than a rule of thumb. It reflects the actual terms of the operating agreement that create the lack of control and marketability in the first place.

The weak file is assembled after the notice of examination. It reads as reverse-engineered to a predetermined answer, and examiners and courts treat it accordingly. Adequate-disclosure rules matter too: a properly disclosed gift starts the statute of limitations, and a poorly disclosed one leaves the valuation open for years.

The through-line is timing. Discounts win when the appraisal, the entity documents, and the disclosure were all in place before the gift. They lose when the planning outran the paper.
